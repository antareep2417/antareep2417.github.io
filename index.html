About

I study how pressurised fluids set faults and fracture networks in motion, and why some of that slip is quiet while some of it is an earthquake.

I am a PhD student at EPFL, working on fluid-driven frictional ruptures in discrete fracture networks, with a focus on fault damage zones. My work combines theory with numerical modelling using a boundary element method (BEM) solver developed in our group. It is relevant to induced seismicity in geo-energy applications such as fluid injection for geothermal energy and subsurface storage.

Before EPFL, I completed an M.S. (Research) in Civil Engineering at IIT Madras, where I developed physics-informed neural network (PINN) methods for heterogeneous subsurface systems, and a B.Tech. in Civil Engineering at NIT Silchar.

Research

Fluid-induced frictional ruptures in fractured rock

Doctoral research · EPFL · 2023 – present

Injecting fluid into the subsurface can reactivate pre-existing faults and fractures. Whether the resulting slip stays slow and aseismic or runs away as a seismic rupture depends on the interplay of fluid pressure diffusion, friction and fracture geometry. I develop theoretical and numerical models of this process in discrete fracture networks, with particular attention to fault damage zones, where closely spaced fractures interact and can set off cascades of slip.

Physics-informed neural networks for interface problems

M.S. thesis · IIT Madras · 2021 – 2023

I developed physics-informed neural network frameworks for problems with material interfaces, which are central to modelling geologically layered and faulted subsurface systems. This led to Interface PINNs (I-PINNs) and Variational Interface PINNs (VI-PINNs), and a collaboration on the adaptive variant, AdaI-PINNs.
