Fluid-induced frictional ruptures in fractured rock

Doctoral research · Geo-Energy Laboratory, EPFL · 2023 – present · Advisor: Prof. Brice Lecampion

Overview

Injecting fluid into the subsurface, as in geothermal energy or subsurface storage, can reactivate pre-existing faults and fractures. The resulting slip can stay slow and aseismic, or it can accelerate into a seismic rupture. Which one happens depends on how fluid pressure diffuses through the fracture network, on fault friction, and on the geometry of the fractures.

The objective of this thesis is to carry out theoretical and numerical studies of the physics of fluid-driven frictional ruptures in discrete fracture networks, with particular attention to fault damage zones. These are regions of closely spaced fractures around major faults, where fractures interact and slip on one can load its neighbours.

Approach

The simulations use a boundary element method (BEM) solver developed in our group, which represents fractures as surfaces in an elastic medium and so avoids meshing the surrounding rock. Fractures are arranged in discrete fracture networks, and fluid pressure and frictional slip are coupled on them.
